The vessel

Rapid-response science platform

Most research ships are floating laboratories. Alberta II is a sprinter — a compact hydrofoil that puts a survey team, an ROV and its air asset on a remote site before the weather window closes.

Built on a long, slender displacement hull with a reverse-raked Nordic bow, she loiters like a conventional vessel. Then the foils extend, the hull lifts clear of the water, and she transits at 45–55 knots. Missions run two to five days: survey and mapping work, environmental monitoring, underwater recovery, and rapid deployment of her embarked vehicles.

The stern deck is deliberately compact — one A-frame, one electric knuckle crane, one winch. Enough to work. Not enough to clutter the build.

Not a floating lab.
A fast platform for short, sharp expeditions.

The agency

The Oceanic Marine Underwater Agency is a multinational body given over to exploration, scientific discovery, underwater recovery and marine research worldwide. Its fleet runs from research ships and deep-diving submersibles to aircraft and specialist transport — every machine built to reach further, dive deeper and arrive sooner. Alberta II is Hull 07, the first to fly.
